What happens after the last cigarette

Time since quittingWhat changes
20 minutesHeart rate drops
24 hoursNicotine in the blood falls to zero
Several daysCarbon monoxide in the blood returns to normal
1 to 12 monthsCoughing and shortness of breath ease
1 to 2 yearsRisk of heart attack drops sharply
5 to 10 yearsAdded risk of mouth, throat and voice-box cancer halves; stroke risk falls
10 to 15 yearsAdded risk of lung cancer halves
15 yearsRisk of coronary heart disease is close to a non-smoker's

Quitting helps at any age. People who stop before 40 avoid about 90% of the extra risk of dying from smoking-related disease.

What doubles your chances

  • Medicines: nicotine replacement (patch plus gum or lozenge), varenicline or bupropion.
  • Support: a quitline, a counsellor, a text programme or an app.
  • Both together work better than either alone.

Most people need several attempts. A slip is information about a trigger, not a failure. Set a new date and go again.

Not quit yet?

Enter a future date and the calculator shows what you will save each week and year.

Before you rely on this number

  • The timeline describes average changes across many people. Individual recovery depends on how long and how much you smoked.
  • Vaping is less harmful than smoking but not harmless, and the long-term effects are still being studied. Talk to a health professional about the best route for you.

Questions people ask

How long do cravings last?

Each craving passes in a few minutes. They are strongest in the first week and fade over the first month.

Will I gain weight?

Many people gain 2 to 5 kg. The health benefit of quitting far outweighs it, and activity and planned snacks limit it.
