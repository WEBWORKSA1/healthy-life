Heart-rate zone calculator

Enter your age and, if you know it, your resting heart rate. Get five zones, your moderate and vigorous ranges, and the easy zone 2 band.

Your entries stay in this browser. Nothing is sent to us.

Step 1: maximum heart rate

  • Tanaka: 208 − (0.7 × age). Based on a review of 351 studies; the better estimate for most adults.
  • Classic: 220 − age. Simple, and still widely used.

Example: at 40, Tanaka gives 180 bpm and the classic formula gives 180 too; at 60 they give 166 and 160.

Step 2: zones

Zone% of maximumUse
150 to 60%Warm-up, recovery
260 to 70%Easy aerobic base; conversation pace
370 to 80%Steady, comfortably hard
480 to 90%Threshold efforts
590 to 100%Short intervals

If you enter a resting heart rate, the calculator switches to the Karvonen method: zone = resting + percentage × (maximum − resting). It reflects your fitness better.

How to use zones

For health, most of your week belongs in zones 2 and 3: the "moderate" intensity that guidelines count. Add a little time in zones 4 and 5 once you have a base. The talk test is a reliable cross-check: full sentences in zone 2, a few words in zone 4.

Before you rely on this number

  • Age formulas can be 10 or more beats out for an individual.
  • Beta-blockers and some other medicines lower heart rate; zones from formulas will not apply. Use effort and breathing instead.
  • If you have a heart condition or get chest pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness, get medical advice before training by heart rate.

Questions people ask

What is zone 2?

Easy aerobic effort at about 60 to 70% of maximum heart rate. You can hold a conversation. It builds endurance with little strain.

How do I find my resting heart rate?

Count your pulse for 60 seconds after sitting quietly for five minutes, ideally in the morning. Most adults are between 60 and 100; fit people are often lower.
