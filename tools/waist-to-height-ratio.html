Waist-to-height ratio

Measure your waist, enter your height and see if you are under the 0.5 line that health bodies use as a marker of lower risk.

Your entries stay in this browser. Nothing is sent to us.

The rule in one line

Keep your waist to less than half your height.

Example: a 170 cm person with an 82 cm waist has a ratio of 82 ÷ 170 = 0.48, inside the healthy band.

Why waist matters more than weight

Fat stored around the organs in your abdomen is more strongly linked with type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease than fat stored elsewhere. Two people with the same BMI can carry very different amounts of it. A tape measure picks that up; a scale cannot.

The bands

RatioWhat it suggests
0.4 to 0.49Healthy central fat level
0.5 to 0.59Increased health risk
0.6 or moreHigh health risk

The UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence recommends this measure for adults with a BMI under 35, and the same bands apply to men and women and across ethnic groups.

How to measure

Stand, breathe out normally and wrap the tape midway between the bottom of your ribs and the top of your hip bones, roughly level with your navel. Keep it snug, not tight, and parallel to the floor.

Before you rely on this number

  • Not reliable in pregnancy, with abdominal swelling from a medical condition, or for children under 5.
  • For people with a BMI over 35 the ratio adds little, because risk is already raised.
  • Measure in the same place each time. A few centimetres' difference in tape position changes the result.

Questions people ask

What is a good waist-to-height ratio?

Between 0.4 and 0.49. In practice: your waist should measure less than half your height.

Is it better than BMI?

For predicting heart and metabolic risk, studies generally find it performs as well or better, because it reflects where fat is carried. Using both gives the best picture.
