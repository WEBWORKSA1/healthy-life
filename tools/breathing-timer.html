Breathing timer

Pick a pattern and a length, press start and follow the circle. One to five minutes is enough to slow your heart rate and settle your mind.

The four patterns

PatternCountGood for
Box breathingIn 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4Focus under pressure
Longer exhaleIn 4, out 6Quick calming, easy for beginners
4-7-8In 4, hold 7, out 8Winding down before sleep
Slow and evenIn 5.5, out 5.5About 5.5 breaths a minute, a steady reset

Why slow breathing works

Breathing is the one part of your stress response you can steer directly. Slowing it to around six breaths a minute, especially with a longer out-breath, activates the vagus nerve and shifts the body towards its rest state. Heart rate falls and heart-rate variability rises. Trials find small to moderate drops in self-reported stress and anxiety after regular practice.

How to do it well

  • Sit or lie comfortably. Loosen your shoulders and jaw.
  • Breathe in through your nose, letting your belly rise.
  • Breathe out gently through your nose or pursed lips.
  • If the holds feel uncomfortable, shorten them or choose the longer-exhale pattern.

Two or three minutes, once or twice a day, is enough to notice a difference within a week or two.

Before you rely on this number

  • Feeling light-headed means you are breathing too deeply. Return to normal breathing and try again more gently.
  • If you have a lung or heart condition, or are pregnant, skip long breath-holds.
  • Breathing exercises help with everyday stress. They are not a treatment for panic disorder, depression or other conditions, which deserve professional care.

Questions people ask

What is box breathing?

A four-part pattern: breathe in, hold, breathe out and hold, each for a count of four. It is taught to people who work under pressure because it is simple to remember.

How long should I do breathing exercises?

Start with two minutes. Five to ten minutes a day gives the most consistent results in studies.
