Daily calorie calculator (TDEE)

Your total daily energy expenditure is the number of calories that would keep your weight where it is. See it for your activity level, with the others alongside.

Your entries stay in this browser. Nothing is sent to us.

How it works

First we estimate your resting needs with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. Then we multiply by an activity factor:

Activity levelFactor
Mostly sitting, little exercise1.2
Light: exercise 1 to 3 days a week1.375
Moderate: exercise 3 to 5 days a week1.55
Very active: hard exercise 6 to 7 days a week1.725
Extra active: physical job plus training1.9

Example: resting needs of 1,500 kcal at the "light" level: 1,500 × 1.375 = about 2,060 kcal a day.

Choosing your level honestly

Activity level is where most estimates go wrong, and nearly always upwards. A desk job plus three gym sessions is usually "light", not "moderate". If you are torn between two, choose the lower and adjust later.

Using the number

Eat near this figure and your weight should hold steady. Check against reality: if your weight drifts over two to three weeks, your true figure is a little higher or lower. Nudge by 100 to 200 kcal and watch again.

Before you rely on this number

  • An estimate, typically within 10 to 15% of your true expenditure.
  • Not for under-18s, pregnancy or breastfeeding, where needs are higher and should be set with a professional.
  • If you have or have had an eating disorder, calorie numbers may not be helpful. Speak to your care team.

Questions people ask

What does TDEE stand for?

Total daily energy expenditure: resting needs plus everything you burn moving, exercising and digesting food.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

A moderate deficit of about 500 kcal a day below your TDEE gives roughly 0.5 kg (1 lb) a week. Intakes below 1,200 kcal for women or 1,500 for men are not advised without medical supervision.
