The formula

Calories = MET × weight in kg × time in hours

A MET (metabolic equivalent) compares an activity with sitting quietly, which is 1 MET. Brisk walking is about 4.3 METs: 4.3 times the energy of sitting.

Example: 70 kg, brisk walking, 30 minutes: 4.3 × 70 × 0.5 = about 150 kcal.

Intensity at a glance

LevelMETsFeels like
LightUnder 3Easy; can sing
Moderate3 to 5.9Can talk, cannot sing
Vigorous6 or moreA few words at a time

Moderate and vigorous minutes count towards the 150 to 300 minutes a week that the WHO recommends. Vigorous minutes count double.

A caution about "burning off" food

The figure is gross: it includes what you would have burned anyway. And bodies compensate, often by moving less later or eating a little more. Exercise is superb for your heart, mood, sleep, strength and blood sugar. As a weight-loss tool on its own it is modest. Value it for everything else it does.

Before you rely on this number

  • MET values are averages measured in adults; fitness, technique, terrain and body composition change real energy use.
  • Wearables that claim calorie precision are often off by 20% or more.

Questions people ask

How many calories does walking burn?

At a moderate pace a 70 kg person uses about 245 kcal an hour; at a brisk pace about 300.

What is a MET?

A unit comparing the energy cost of an activity with resting. One MET is roughly 1 kcal per kg of body weight per hour.
