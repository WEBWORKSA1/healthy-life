BMR calculator

Your basal metabolic rate is the energy you would use lying still all day. It is the base that every calorie estimate is built on.

Your entries stay in this browser. Nothing is sent to us.

The formula

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which most dietitians treat as the most reliable simple estimate for adults:

  • Men: (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
  • Women: (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161

Example: a 35-year-old woman, 165 cm, 65 kg: 650 + 1,031 − 175 − 161 = 1,345 kcal a day.

What BMR is and is not

About 60 to 70% of the energy you use in a day goes on staying alive: heart, lungs, brain, liver, kidneys, and keeping warm. That is your BMR. Movement and digesting food make up the rest.

BMR is not a target to eat. Eating at or below it for long is not recommended without medical supervision. For a practical number, multiply by your activity level in the daily calorie calculator.

What changes it

Bigger and more muscular bodies burn more. BMR falls slowly with age, mostly because muscle is lost, which is one reason strength training matters as you get older.

Before you rely on this number

  • Equations are accurate to within about 10% for most people, and less so at very high or low body weights.
  • Thyroid conditions, some medicines, illness and pregnancy change resting energy use.
  • Only a lab test (indirect calorimetry) measures it directly.

Questions people ask

What is a normal BMR?

Most adults fall between about 1,200 and 2,000 kcal a day. It depends mainly on body size, sex and age.

Can I raise my BMR?

Modestly. Building or keeping muscle is the main lever. Crash dieting tends to lower it.

Sources
