Sleep calculator

Tell us when you need to wake up, or when you are going to bed. We count back or forward in 90-minute cycles and flag the options that give you enough sleep for your age.

Your entries stay in this browser. Nothing is sent to us.

How it works

Sleep runs in cycles of light, deep and REM sleep, each lasting about 90 minutes. Waking at the end of a cycle, in light sleep, tends to feel easier than being dragged out of deep sleep.

The calculator takes your target time, allows for the minutes you need to fall asleep, and lists the times that fit 6, 5, 4 or 3 full cycles.

Example: to wake at 7:00 am with 15 minutes to fall asleep, be in bed by 9:45 pm (6 cycles, 9 hours) or 11:15 pm (5 cycles, 7.5 hours).

How much you need

AgeRecommended sleep a night
13 to 188 to 10 hours
Adults7 or more hours
65 and over7 to 8 hours

These come from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the U.S. National Sleep Foundation. Regularly sleeping under 7 hours is linked with higher risk of weight gain, high blood pressure, diabetes, heart disease and low mood.

The habit that beats any calculator

A fixed wake-up time, seven days a week. It anchors your body clock, and a steady bedtime follows by itself within a week or two.

Before you rely on this number

  • The 90-minute cycle is an average. Yours may be 70 to 120 minutes and changes through the night.
  • Timing cannot fix too little sleep. Four cycles is a short night however well it is timed.
  • Loud snoring with pauses in breathing, or insomnia lasting more than three months, should be checked by a doctor.

Questions people ask

What time should I go to bed?

Count back 7.5 or 9 hours from your wake-up time, then add the 10 to 20 minutes it takes you to fall asleep.

Is 6 hours of sleep enough?

For most adults, no. A small number of people do well on it, but 7 or more is the recommendation.
