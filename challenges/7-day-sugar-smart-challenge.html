Nourish challenge

7-day sugar-smart challenge

This is not a no-sugar week. It is seven days of noticing where added sugar comes from in your own diet and making one swap at a time. Fruit, plain milk and plain yoghurt are not the target.

Your progress is saved in this browser only.

How it works

  • Added sugar is what is put in during processing or at the table. Whole fruit does not count.
  • One swap a day. Keep the ones that were painless.
  • Read the 'added sugars' or 'of which sugars' line on labels.
  • Not suitable as a weight-loss diet. If you have diabetes, keep following your care plan.

Day by day

  1. Day 1 List everything sweet you drink in a day. Drinks are the biggest single source for most people.
  2. Day 2 Swap one sugary drink for water, sparkling water or unsweetened tea.
  3. Day 3 Read the label on your breakfast cereal, yoghurt or bread. Find one with less added sugar.
  4. Day 4 Check your sauces: ketchup, pasta sauce and dressings. Pick one lower-sugar option.
  5. Day 5 Have fruit, nuts or plain yoghurt in place of one sweet snack.
  6. Day 6 Halve the sugar or syrup in your tea, coffee or porridge.
  7. Day 7 Decide which three swaps you will keep. Write them down.

  • Water intake calculatorHow much to drink a day, adjusted for exercise and heat.

Questions people ask

How much added sugar is too much?

The WHO advises keeping free sugars under 10% of daily energy, and says under 5% (about 25 g or 6 teaspoons) brings extra benefit. The 2025–2030 U.S. guidelines say no amount is needed in a healthy diet.

Are sweeteners better?

They cut calories, but evidence on long-term benefit is mixed, and the WHO advises against relying on them for weight control. Water and unsweetened drinks are the simplest choice.
