Editorial policy

How content and tools are made, checked and paid for.

Last updated 19 November 2026.

Sources

We build on guidelines and evidence reviews from recognised bodies, in this order of preference:

  1. International and national health authorities: the World Health Organization, the U.S. CDC, NIH and FDA, the UK's NHS and NICE, Health Canada and their equivalents.
  2. Professional societies such as the American Heart Association, the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the American Diabetes Association.
  3. Systematic reviews and large, well-conducted studies in peer-reviewed journals.

We do not base advice on single small studies, press releases, product marketing or personal testimony. Every guide and tool lists its sources and the date it was last checked.

Writing and checking

  • Content is written by the Healthy.Life editorial team in plain language and checked line by line against the cited sources before publication.
  • Where authorities disagree, for example on protein targets or alcohol limits, we present the positions and say that they differ.
  • We avoid absolute promises, fear-based headlines and before-and-after imagery.
  • A page carries a named, credentialed reviewer only when one has reviewed it. Otherwise it has been checked against sources by the editorial team, not by a clinician.
  • Drafting and coding may be assisted by software tools, including AI. A person is responsible for every published page and for checking it against the sources.

Calculators

  • Each tool uses a published formula, names it and links to its origin.
  • Results are shown with guideline ranges and a plain statement of what the tool cannot see.
  • Safety limits are built in: calorie targets have floors, underweight goals are refused, and severe readings trigger a prompt to seek care.
  • Tools are for adults unless stated, and are estimates for education. They do not diagnose.

Updates and corrections

Guidelines change. We review pages when a source is updated and at least once a year. If you spot an error, tell us through the contact form. We correct confirmed errors promptly and change the "last reviewed" date.

Automated sections

The Health Pulse and video pages are assembled automatically from the public feeds of the organisations listed on those pages. We show the publisher's headline, source name, date and a link. We do not copy articles, and we do not endorse or verify the linked content. Food recall listings come from the U.S. FDA's open data service.

How we make money

Healthy.Life is free to use. It is paid for in five ways:

  1. Advertising. Display ads are served by Google AdSense and are labelled "Advertisement" where we place them. We do not choose individual ads, and we block categories we consider unsuitable for a health site where the system allows.
  2. Sponsorship. A sponsor may be named beside a tool, challenge or contest. Sponsored placements are labelled. Our standards are on the advertise page.
  3. Introductions. If you ask to be introduced to a professional or programme when you build a plan, we may receive a fee from them. You are never charged, and the plan is the same whether or not you ask.
  4. Affiliate links. If a page links to a product and we may earn a commission, the page says so next to the link.
  5. Reader support. Voluntary contributions through the support page.

Advertisers, sponsors, partners and supporters have no influence over calculations, editorial content, source selection or contest judging.

Reader stories and contests

Stories are published only with permission, by first name and region. We do not publish invented testimonials, and we do not accept stories that promote unsafe practices or make medical claims.
