## Recommended sleep by age

| Age | Hours in 24 |
| --- | --- |
| 4 to 12 months | 12 to 16, including naps |
| 1 to 2 years | 11 to 14, including naps |
| 3 to 5 years | 10 to 13, including naps |
| 6 to 12 years | 9 to 12 |
| 13 to 18 years | 8 to 10 |
| Adults | 7 or more |

These come from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine. Most adults land between 7 and 9 hours. A small minority function well on a little less, and they are far rarer than the number of people who believe they are among them.

## Are you getting enough?

Hours in bed are not hours asleep. Signs that you are short:

- You need an alarm every day and feel groggy for more than 30 minutes.
- You fall asleep within five minutes of lying down.
- You rely on caffeine to get through the afternoon.
- You sleep two or more hours longer at weekends.
- You doze off in meetings, on the sofa or, most seriously, at the wheel.

## What short sleep costs

About one adult in three regularly sleeps less than 7 hours. Over time that is linked with higher risk of weight gain, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, depression and accidents. In the short term it dulls attention, memory, mood and immune response. After 17 to 19 hours awake, reaction times resemble those of someone at the drink-drive limit used in many countries.

## Quality and timing

Seven broken hours are not the same as seven solid ones. Good sleep means falling asleep within about 20 minutes, waking no more than briefly, and feeling restored.

Timing matters too. People with irregular sleep schedules have worse metabolic and heart health than regular sleepers with the same average hours. A fixed wake-up time is the single most useful rule.

## Can you catch up?

A lie-in or a nap takes the edge off one bad night. It does not undo weeks of short sleep, and long weekend lie-ins shift your body clock, making Monday harder. Adding 30 to 60 minutes a night works better than bingeing at the weekend.

## When to see a doctor

Loud snoring with pauses in breathing, an urge to move your legs at night, falling asleep suddenly in the day, or trouble sleeping at least three nights a week for three months. Effective treatments exist for all of these.
