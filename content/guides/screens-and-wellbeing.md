## What we know

Research on screen time and wellbeing is less alarming, and less clear, than headlines suggest. Across large studies the average link between total screen hours and adult wellbeing is small. What stands out is **what screens replace** and **how they are used**:

- Screens that displace sleep, exercise or time with people are a problem.
- Passive scrolling and social comparison are linked with lower mood; messaging friends, learning and creating are not.
- Late-night use delays sleep, through light and through stimulation.

So the question is less "how many hours?" than "what did it cost me?".

## Six adjustments worth making

1. **Phone out of the bedroom.** Buy an alarm clock. This single change improves sleep for many people.
2. **A screen-free first and last half hour** of the day.
3. **Turn off non-human notifications.** Keep calls and messages from people; silence apps.
4. **Move the tempting apps** off your home screen or log out after each use. A few seconds of friction cuts use.
5. **Meals without screens.** You eat more slowly and notice when you are full.
6. **Use the 20-20-20 rule for your eyes:** every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds.

## Posture and movement

A day at a screen is a day of sitting. Raise the screen to eye level, keep elbows near 90 degrees and feet flat, and stand up every 30 minutes. A stiff neck and tight shoulders are usually about stillness more than posture.

## An honest audit

Your phone's screen-time report shows where the hours go. Pick the one app that leaves you feeling worst and set a daily limit for it. Notice how you feel after a week.

## Children and teenagers

Evidence is stronger for younger people: heavy use is associated with less sleep and less physical activity. Many paediatric bodies advise no screens for under-2s apart from video calls, limited and co-viewed use for young children, and screen-free bedrooms at night for all ages. Agreed family rules work better than bans.

## When to get help

If you cannot cut back despite trying, or screen use is harming work, study, relationships or sleep, talk to a doctor. Gambling and gaming problems in particular respond to treatment.
