## What counts

**Added** or **free** sugars are those put into food and drinks by manufacturers, cooks or you, plus the sugars in honey, syrups and fruit juice. Sugar inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain milk does not count, because it comes packaged with fibre, water and nutrients and is absorbed more slowly.

## The limits

| Source | Advice |
| --- | --- |
| World Health Organization | Under 10% of daily energy; under 5% (about 25 g) for extra benefit |
| U.S. Dietary Guidelines, 2025–2030 | No amount is needed; no more than 10 g at a meal |
| American Heart Association | 25 g a day for women, 36 g for men |

For someone eating 2,000 kcal, 10% is 50 g: about 12 teaspoons. A 355 ml (12 oz) can of regular cola has around 39 g.

## Where it comes from

1. Sugary drinks: soft drinks, energy drinks, sweetened coffee and tea, fruit drinks.
2. Sweets, biscuits, cakes, ice cream.
3. Breakfast cereals and flavoured yoghurts.
4. Sauces and condiments: ketchup, barbecue sauce, some pasta sauces and dressings.

The last two groups are the ones people miss.

## Reading the label

Look for "added sugars" (U.S.) or "of which sugars" (UK and EU). Divide grams by 4 for teaspoons. On ingredient lists, sugar hides under many names: sucrose, glucose syrup, high-fructose corn syrup, dextrose, maltose, cane juice, agave, honey. If one appears in the first three ingredients, the product is mostly sugar.

## Swaps that stick

- Sparkling water with a squeeze of citrus for soft drinks.
- Plain yoghurt with fruit for flavoured yoghurt.
- Porridge with banana and cinnamon for sweetened cereal.
- Half the sugar in tea and coffee this month, half again next month. Taste adapts in a few weeks.
- Fruit, nuts or a square of dark chocolate after a meal.

## Why bother

High intakes of added sugar, especially from drinks, are linked with weight gain, type 2 diabetes, heart disease, fatty liver and tooth decay. You do not have to reach zero. Cutting drinks alone gets most people most of the way.
