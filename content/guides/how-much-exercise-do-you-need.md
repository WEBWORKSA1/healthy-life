## The target

The World Health Organization's guideline for adults, including those over 65 and those living with long-term conditions, is:

| What | How much each week |
| --- | --- |
| Moderate aerobic activity | 150 to 300 minutes |
| **or** vigorous aerobic activity | 75 to 150 minutes |
| Muscle strengthening, all major muscle groups | 2 or more days |
| Balance and functional training (65 and over) | 3 or more days |

One vigorous minute counts as two moderate ones, so you can mix them.

## What counts

Moderate means you can talk but not sing: brisk walking, easy cycling, water aerobics, dancing, pushing a mower. Vigorous means a few words at a time: running, fast cycling, football, lap swimming, carrying heavy loads uphill.

Bouts no longer need to last 10 minutes. Climbing stairs, a fast walk to the bus and energetic housework all add up.

## What you get for it

Regular activity lowers the risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, several cancers, depression and dementia. It improves sleep, blood pressure, mood and thinking, often from the first session. Few medicines do as much.

The relationship is a curve, not a cliff. Going from nothing to 10 minutes a day cuts risk more than going from 150 to 300 minutes a week.

## Getting there from zero

- **Weeks 1 and 2:** 10 minutes of brisk walking a day.
- **Weeks 3 and 4:** 20 minutes on five days.
- **Weeks 5 and 6:** 30 minutes on five days. That is 150.
- Add two short strength sessions once walking feels routine.

Pick things you do not dread, attach them to something you already do, and expect to miss days. A missed day changes nothing; a missed month does.

## When to check with a doctor first

If you have chest pain, dizziness or breathlessness on mild effort, or a heart, lung or joint condition, ask how to start. For most people the risk of staying inactive is far greater than the risk of starting gently.
