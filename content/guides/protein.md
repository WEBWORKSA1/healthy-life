## How much

| Situation | Grams per kg a day | For a 70 kg person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum (RDA) | 0.8 | 56 g |
| Adults, 2025–2030 U.S. guidelines | 1.2 to 1.6 | 84 to 112 g |
| Strength training to build muscle | 1.6 to 2.2 | 112 to 154 g |
| Over 65 | 1.2 to 1.6 | 84 to 112 g |

The 0.8 g figure prevents deficiency; it was never meant as an optimum. The newest U.S. guidelines moved the suggested range up. Some researchers think that is more than inactive younger adults need. There is broad agreement that older adults and people who train benefit from the higher range, and that these amounts are safe for healthy kidneys.

## When

Muscle uses protein best in moderate doses. Most people eat little at breakfast and a lot at dinner. Evening it out, to roughly 25 to 35 g a meal, supports muscle and helps you feel full through the day.

## From what

| Food | Protein |
| --- | --- |
| Chicken breast, 100 g cooked | 31 g |
| Canned tuna, 100 g | 25 g |
| Greek yoghurt, 170 g | 17 g |
| Firm tofu, 100 g | 17 g |
| Lentils, 150 g cooked | 13 g |
| Eggs, 2 large | 12 g |
| Peanut butter, 2 tablespoons | 7 g |

The package matters. Fish brings omega-3 fats; pulses bring fibre; nuts bring unsaturated fat. Processed meats such as bacon, ham and sausages are linked with bowel cancer and heart disease and are best kept occasional.

Plant proteins are slightly less concentrated and some are low in one amino acid or another, but eating a variety across the day covers everything. Well-planned vegetarian and vegan diets meet protein needs.

## Do you need supplements?

No. Powders are convenient, not superior. If you use one, choose a product tested by an independent lab, and count it as food, not as an extra.

## Who should be careful

People with chronic kidney disease need individual limits and should not raise protein without medical advice.
