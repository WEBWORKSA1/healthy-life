## How the advice changed

For years moderate drinking was thought to protect the heart. Newer analyses show much of that apparent benefit came from flawed comparisons: many "non-drinkers" in older studies had stopped because they were ill. With that corrected, the benefit largely disappears, while the cancer risk remains.

| Body | Current position |
| --- | --- |
| World Health Organization | No level of alcohol consumption is safe for health |
| Canada (2023) | 0 drinks: no risk. 1 to 2 a week: low. 3 to 6: moderate. 7 or more: increasingly high |
| United Kingdom | No more than 14 units a week, spread over 3 or more days, with drink-free days |
| United States (2025–2030) | Drink less for better health; some people should not drink at all |

## What alcohol does

Alcohol is a cause of at least seven cancers, including breast, bowel, mouth, throat, oesophagus and liver. Risk rises from the first drink and climbs with the amount. It also raises blood pressure, damages the liver, disrupts sleep, adds calories and is involved in a large share of injuries.

## What counts as a drink

| Drink | Approximate alcohol |
| --- | --- |
| Beer, 355 ml at 5% | 14 g: 1 U.S. drink, 1.8 UK units |
| Wine, 150 ml at 12% | 14 g: 1 U.S. drink, 1.8 UK units |
| Spirits, 44 ml at 40% | 14 g: 1 U.S. drink, 1.8 UK units |
| Wine, large 250 ml glass at 13% | 26 g: 3.3 UK units |
| Beer, UK pint at 5% | 22 g: 2.8 UK units |

Home pours are usually bigger than standard measures. [Add up your week](/tools/alcohol-units-calculator.html).

## Cutting down

- Set a weekly limit and count as you go.
- Have at least two or three alcohol-free days.
- Alternate with water or an alcohol-free drink.
- Choose smaller glasses and lower-strength versions.
- Do not keep much at home.
- Plan what you will say: "Not tonight, thanks" is enough.
- Notice your triggers and change the routine around them.

A month off is a useful experiment. Many people report better sleep, energy and mood, and drink less afterwards.

## Who should not drink

Anyone who is pregnant or trying to conceive, under the legal age, driving or operating machinery, taking medicines that interact, or unable to control their drinking.

## If stopping feels hard

If you drink heavily every day, do not stop abruptly; withdrawal can be dangerous. A doctor can arrange a safe plan. If you worry about your drinking or someone else's, a doctor or a national alcohol helpline is a confidential place to start.
