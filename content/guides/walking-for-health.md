## Why walking is enough

Walking needs no kit, no membership and no skill. It strengthens the heart, lowers blood pressure and blood sugar, protects joints by keeping them moving, and lifts mood. In long-term studies, regular walkers live longer and have less heart disease, and the benefit begins well below 10,000 steps.

## How brisk is brisk?

You should be able to talk, but not sing. If you like numbers, about **100 steps a minute** is moderate intensity for most adults. Count your steps for 15 seconds: 25 or more and you are there.

A slower stroll is still worth doing. It breaks up sitting and counts as light activity.

## The four-week plan

| Week | Most days | Weekly total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 10 minutes | 50 to 70 minutes |
| 2 | 15 minutes | 75 to 105 minutes |
| 3 | 20 minutes | 100 to 140 minutes |
| 4 | 30 minutes, 5 days | 150 minutes |

Split a walk in two if that fits your day. Once 30 minutes feels easy, add hills, pick up the pace for one minute in every five, or lengthen one walk at the weekend.

## Technique, briefly

Stand tall, look ahead, relax your shoulders and let your arms swing. Land on your heel and roll through to push off from your toes. Comfortable shoes with a little cushioning are all you need.

## Making it stick

- Walk at the same time each day: after lunch or dinner works for most people.
- Take calls on foot.
- Walk with someone. You get connection as a bonus.
- Track it. The [30-day walking challenge](/challenges/30-day-walking-challenge.html) gives you a box to tick.

## A short walk after meals

Ten to fifteen minutes of walking after eating blunts the rise in blood sugar. It is one of the simplest habits with a measurable effect.
