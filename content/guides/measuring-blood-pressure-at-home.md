## Why measure at home

Blood pressure in a clinic is a snapshot, often pushed up by nerves ("white-coat effect") or occasionally falsely reassuring. A week of home readings gives a truer average and predicts risk better. Guidelines in the U.S., UK and Europe all recommend home monitoring to confirm high blood pressure and to follow treatment.

## Choosing a monitor

- **Upper-arm, automatic.** Wrist and finger devices are less reliable.
- **Validated.** Check your country's list of clinically validated devices.
- **Right cuff size.** A cuff that is too small reads high. Measure around your upper arm and match it to the range printed on the cuff.

## The seven steps

1. **Prepare.** No caffeine, exercise or smoking for 30 minutes beforehand. Empty your bladder.
2. **Sit.** Back supported, feet flat on the floor, legs uncrossed. Rest for five minutes.
3. **Position.** Arm bare, supported on a table so the cuff is level with your heart, palm up.
4. **Cuff.** Snug, with its lower edge two fingers above the elbow crease.
5. **Stay quiet.** Do not talk or look at your phone.
6. **Measure twice,** one minute apart.
7. **Record** both readings with the date and time.

Each mistake matters. Talking, an unsupported arm, crossed legs or a full bladder can each add 5 to 15 points.

## When and how often

For a diagnosis or a treatment change: twice in the morning before medicines and breakfast, and twice in the evening, for seven days. Discard day one and average the rest. After that, a few readings a month is plenty unless your doctor asks for more.

## Reading the average

| Home average | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Under 120/80 | Normal |
| 120–129 and under 80 | Elevated |
| 130/80 or higher | High by U.S. criteria |
| 135/85 or higher | High by UK and European home criteria |

Bring your record, and your monitor once a year, to appointments so it can be checked against the clinic's.

## When to act straight away

A reading above 180/120, repeated after a minute of rest, needs same-day medical advice. With chest pain, breathlessness, weakness, numbness, vision change or difficulty speaking, call emergency services.
