## What a short nap does

A 10 to 20 minute nap improves alertness, mood and reaction time for a couple of hours, with little grogginess. That is why pilots, shift workers and drivers are advised to use them.

## Length matters

| Nap length | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 10 to 20 minutes | Light sleep only. Wake refreshed. |
| 30 to 60 minutes | You reach deep sleep. Waking from it brings 15 to 30 minutes of grogginess, called sleep inertia. |
| 90 minutes | A full cycle. Useful after a very short night, but likely to push back bedtime. |

For most people, most days, 20 minutes is the sweet spot. Set an alarm for 25 to 30 minutes to allow for dropping off.

## Timing

The early afternoon dip, roughly 1 to 3 pm, is when the body is most ready to nap. Later naps eat into the sleep pressure you need at night.

## How to nap

- Find somewhere quiet, dim and cool. An eye mask and earplugs help.
- Lie down if you can; a reclined seat is second best.
- Do not worry about falling asleep. Quiet rest with eyes closed has benefits too.
- A coffee just before a 20-minute nap is a well-tested trick: the caffeine arrives as you wake.

## When naps are a bad idea

If you have trouble falling or staying asleep at night, skip naps for a couple of weeks and see whether nights improve. Naps reduce the drive to sleep.

## When naps are a warning sign

Occasional naps are normal. Check with a doctor if:

- You need long naps most days even after 7 to 9 hours at night.
- You fall asleep without meaning to.
- A new need to nap comes with snoring, low mood or other symptoms.

Sleep apnoea, depression, thyroid problems, anaemia and some medicines can all cause daytime sleepiness. In older adults, long and frequent naps are associated with poorer health; they are usually a marker of something else rather than a cause.
