## How it works

Through the day a chemical called adenosine builds up in your brain and creates sleep pressure. Caffeine blocks adenosine's receptors, so you feel alert. The adenosine is still there; when caffeine wears off, the tiredness returns.

## How long it lasts

Caffeine peaks in the blood 30 to 60 minutes after you drink it. Its half-life averages about 5 hours and ranges from under 3 to over 9, depending on genes, liver function, smoking (shorter), pregnancy and some medicines (longer).

| Hours after a 200 mg coffee | Caffeine remaining (5-hour half-life) |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | 100 mg |
| 8 | 66 mg |
| 10 | 50 mg |
| 15 | 25 mg |

## What it does to sleep

In a sleep-laboratory study, 400 mg of caffeine taken 6 hours before bed reduced sleep by more than an hour. Participants did not perceive much difference, which is the trap: you can sleep "fine" and still lose deep sleep.

Later reviews suggest stopping ordinary coffee about 9 hours before bed and large doses, such as pre-workout supplements, about 13 hours before.

## Keeping the benefits

Coffee and tea are not the villains. In moderate amounts they are linked with lower risk of type 2 diabetes, heart disease and some other conditions, and caffeine reliably improves alertness and exercise performance.

- **Daily ceiling:** up to 400 mg for most healthy adults; under 200 mg if pregnant.
- **Front-load it:** have most of your caffeine before noon.
- **Delay the first cup** 60 to 90 minutes after waking if you get an afternoon crash; some people find it helps.
- **Switch after lunch** to decaf, herbal tea or water.
- **Watch hidden sources:** energy drinks, cola, dark chocolate, some pain relievers and pre-workout powders.

## Cutting down

Stopping suddenly can bring headaches, tiredness and irritability for a few days. Reduce by about a quarter each week, or swap one cup at a time for decaf.

## Who should be more careful

People with anxiety, reflux, heart-rhythm problems or insomnia, anyone pregnant or breastfeeding, and teenagers. Energy drinks are not recommended for children.
