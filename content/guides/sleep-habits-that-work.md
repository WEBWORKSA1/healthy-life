## The ten, ranked

1. **Fix your wake-up time.** Seven days a week. It anchors your body clock; bedtime sorts itself out.
2. **Get daylight early.** Ten to thirty minutes outdoors in the first hour or two after waking sets the clock for the night ahead.
3. **Cut caffeine by early afternoon.** At least 8 hours before bed.
4. **Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and cool.** Around 18°C (65°F) suits most people. Blackout blinds, earplugs and a fan are cheap fixes.
5. **Wind down for 20 to 30 minutes.** The same sequence each night: dim lights, wash, read, stretch or breathe slowly.
6. **Keep the bed for sleep and sex.** Not work, not scrolling. Your brain should link bed with sleeping.
7. **Go easy on alcohol.** It helps you drop off, then fragments the second half of the night.
8. **Finish big meals and hard exercise 2 to 3 hours before bed.** Regular exercise itself improves sleep.
9. **Park your worries.** Write tomorrow's to-do list early in the evening.
10. **Limit naps** to 20 to 30 minutes before mid-afternoon if nights are a problem.

## The 20-minute rule

Lying awake teaches your brain that bed is a place for being awake. If sleep has not come after roughly 20 minutes, get up, go to another room and do something quiet in dim light. Return when you feel sleepy. Repeat as needed. It is tedious for a few nights and then it works.

## What about screens?

Bright light late at night delays your body clock, and absorbing content keeps your mind switched on. Dimming screens, using night mode and stopping 30 to 60 minutes before bed all help. The content matters at least as much as the light: a calm show is less of a problem than work email or an argument online.

## Do not chase perfect

Clock-watching and sleep trackers can turn a bad night into an anxious one. One poor night does little harm; your body compensates the next night with deeper sleep. Keep your wake time, get outside and carry on.

## If habits are not enough

Good habits fix a lot, but not chronic insomnia. The recommended first treatment is cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), a short structured programme that works better long term than sleeping pills. Ask your doctor, or look for a reputable digital programme.
