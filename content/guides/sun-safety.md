## Why it matters

Ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun and from tanning beds is the main cause of skin cancer, the most common cancer in many countries, and of most visible skin ageing. Damage adds up over a lifetime, and sunburn in childhood raises risk further. Most skin cancers are preventable.

## Know the UV index

The UV index measures the strength of sunburn-causing radiation.

| UV index | Level | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 2 | Low | No protection needed for most people |
| 3 to 5 | Moderate | Seek shade at midday; cover up; sunscreen |
| 6 to 7 | High | Protection essential |
| 8 to 10 | Very high | Extra care; avoid midday sun |
| 11+ | Extreme | Stay out of the midday sun |

Weather apps show it. UV is strongest between about 10 am and 4 pm, at altitude, near the equator, and on water, sand and snow, which reflect it. Cloud blocks less than people think.

## The order of protection

1. **Shade,** especially in the middle of the day.
2. **Clothing:** long sleeves, a wide-brimmed hat, tightly woven fabric.
3. **Sunglasses** that block UVA and UVB.
4. **Sunscreen** on everything still exposed.

## Using sunscreen properly

- **Broad-spectrum, SPF 30 or higher,** water-resistant if you swim or sweat.
- **Enough:** about 35 ml, a shot-glass full, for an adult body. Most people use half of what they need.
- **Early:** 15 to 20 minutes before going out.
- **Again:** every two hours, and after swimming, sweating or towelling.
- **Everywhere:** ears, neck, scalp parting, tops of feet, backs of hands.

SPF 30 filters about 97% of UVB and SPF 50 about 98%. No sunscreen blocks everything, and a high number is not a licence to stay out longer.

## Tanning

A tan is the skin's response to DNA damage. Tanning beds are classed as carcinogenic to humans. If you want the colour, use a self-tan product.

## Vitamin D

Skin makes vitamin D in sunlight, but brief everyday exposure is enough for most people in summer, and food and supplements can cover the rest. You do not need to burn or tan to make it.

## Check your skin

Once a month, look for the ABCDE signs in moles: **A**symmetry, irregular **B**order, uneven **C**olour, **D**iameter over 6 mm, or **E**volving size, shape or colour. Show a doctor anything new, changing, bleeding or that will not heal. People with fair skin, many moles or a family history of melanoma should ask about regular checks.

Darker skin burns less easily, but skin cancer still occurs and is often found later, including on palms, soles and under nails.
