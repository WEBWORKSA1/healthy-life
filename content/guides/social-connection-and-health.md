## The evidence

A pooled analysis of 148 studies found that people with stronger social relationships were about 50% more likely to be alive at follow-up than those with weaker ones. Loneliness and isolation are linked with higher risk of heart disease, stroke, dementia, depression and early death.

The World Health Organization's Commission on Social Connection reported in 2025 that about **one in six people** worldwide experiences loneliness, with the highest rates among young people and in lower-income countries.

## Why it affects the body

- **Stress buffering.** Support dampens the body's stress response.
- **Behaviour.** Connected people are more active, sleep better and are more likely to see a doctor when something is wrong.
- **Meaning.** Feeling needed gives people a reason to look after themselves.

Loneliness is not the same as being alone. It is the gap between the connection you have and the connection you want. Some people with few contacts are content; some surrounded by people are lonely.

## Three kinds of connection

- **Close ties:** the two or three people you could call at 3 am.
- **Friends and family:** the wider circle you see and talk to.
- **Community:** neighbours, colleagues, clubs, congregations, the person at the café.

All three contribute. Brief friendly exchanges with acquaintances lift mood more than people expect.

## Small steps that work

- Send a two-line message to someone you miss. People underestimate how much it is appreciated.
- Make one contact recurring: a weekly call, a monthly dinner, a Saturday walk.
- Join something that meets regularly around an interest. Shared activity makes conversation easy.
- Volunteer. Helping others is one of the most reliable ways to feel connected.
- Combine it with movement: walk with a friend and you cover two pillars at once.

The [7-day reach-out challenge](/challenges/7-day-reach-out-challenge.html) turns these into one small task a day.

## If you are lonely now

It is common, and it says nothing about your worth. Start with low-pressure settings: a class, a walking group, a library event. If loneliness comes with low mood most days, speak to a doctor. Many areas have social-prescribing or community-connector services that link people with local groups.

## For someone else

Ask, listen and follow up. An invitation with a specific time ("Coffee on Thursday at ten?") lands better than "we should catch up".
