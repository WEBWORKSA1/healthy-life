## The idea

Your heart rate rises with effort in a fairly straight line. Dividing that range into zones gives you a way to pace exercise: easy enough to recover from, hard enough to improve.

| Zone | % of maximum heart rate | Talk test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 50 to 60% | Could chat all day |
| 2 | 60 to 70% | Full sentences |
| 3 | 70 to 80% | Short sentences |
| 4 | 80 to 90% | A few words |
| 5 | 90 to 100% | Cannot talk |

Estimate maximum heart rate as 208 − (0.7 × age), or let the [zone calculator](/tools/heart-rate-zones.html) do it.

## Why zone 2 gets so much attention

At easy effort your muscles burn mostly fat with plenty of oxygen. Training there builds the small blood vessels and mitochondria that underpin endurance, with little strain, so you can do a lot of it and recover quickly. It is also the intensity that health guidelines call "moderate".

Zone 2 is not magic. Harder efforts raise fitness faster per minute. The reason coaches favour lots of easy work is that it is repeatable.

## A sensible week

Endurance athletes typically spend about 80% of their training time easy and 20% hard. A version for ordinary life:

- **3 or 4 sessions** in zones 2 and 3: brisk walks, easy rides or jogs, 30 to 45 minutes.
- **1 session** with short hard efforts: for example 6 × 1 minute fast with 2 minutes easy, after a warm-up.
- **2 sessions** of strength work.

## Without a monitor

Use breathing. If you can speak in full sentences, you are in zone 2. If you can manage only a few words, you are in zone 4. Wrist sensors are good at steady efforts and less reliable during intervals; a chest strap is more accurate.

## Cautions

Formulas can be 10 beats or more out for an individual. Beta-blockers and some other medicines hold heart rate down, so zones will not apply. If exercise brings chest pain, faintness or unusual breathlessness, stop and seek medical advice.
