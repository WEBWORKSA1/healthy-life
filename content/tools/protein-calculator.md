## The numbers behind the range

| Who | Grams per kg of body weight a day |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum to prevent deficiency (RDA) | 0.8 |
| Adults, 2025–2030 U.S. Dietary Guidelines | 1.2 to 1.6 |
| Building muscle with strength training | 1.6 to 2.2 |
| Regular endurance training | 1.4 to 1.8 |
| Aged 65 and over | 1.2 to 1.6, spread across meals |

**Example:** at 70 kg, general health: 84 to 112 g a day, or roughly 28 to 37 g at each of three meals.

The 0.8 g figure is a floor, not a goal. The 2025–2030 U.S. guidelines raised the suggested range for adults; some nutrition scientists think that goes further than the evidence requires for people who are not training or older. Either way, amounts in these ranges are safe for healthy adults.

## What that looks like in food

- 100 g cooked chicken breast: about 31 g
- 100 g firm tofu: about 17 g
- 170 g plain Greek yoghurt: about 17 g
- 1 large egg: about 6 g
- 150 g cooked lentils: about 13 g
- 100 g canned tuna: about 25 g

Plant and animal sources both count. Variety across the day covers all the amino acids you need.
