## How it works

- **Stride length** is estimated as 41.4% of your height.
- **Distance** = steps × stride length.
- **Time** = distance ÷ walking speed (3.2, 4.8 or 5.6 km/h).
- **Calories** = MET × weight in kg × hours, using 2.8, 3.5 or 4.3 METs.

**Example:** 7,000 steps at 170 cm: stride 0.70 m, distance 4.9 km (3.1 miles), about an hour at an average pace.

## How many steps are enough?

There is nothing special about 10,000; it started as the name of a 1960s Japanese pedometer. Large studies following people for years find that risk of early death falls steadily as steps rise, with most of the benefit reached by roughly 7,000 to 8,000 a day for adults, and somewhat fewer for people over 60. Going from 3,000 to 5,000 does more for you than going from 10,000 to 12,000.

Pace matters as well. Some of your steps should be brisk enough to raise your breathing.

## Easy ways to add 2,000 steps

A 20-minute walk does it. So do walking calls, getting off a stop early, parking further away and a ten-minute loop after dinner.
