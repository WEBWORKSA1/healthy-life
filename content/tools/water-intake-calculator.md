## The baseline

The U.S. National Academies set an adequate intake for **total** water, from all drinks and food:

| | Total water a day | Of which from drinks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Men | 3.7 L (125 fl oz) | about 3.0 L (13 cups) |
| Women | 2.7 L (91 fl oz) | about 2.2 L (9 cups) |
| Pregnancy | 3.0 L | about 2.3 L |
| Breastfeeding | 3.8 L | about 3.1 L |

Food supplies around a fifth of your water, which is why the drinks figure is lower.

## What we add

- **Exercise:** about 0.6 L for each hour, within the range sports-medicine bodies suggest for sweat replacement.
- **Hot or humid weather:** 0.5 L.

## What counts

Water, milk, tea, coffee, sparkling water and other unsweetened drinks all count. Caffeinated drinks do not dehydrate you at normal intakes. Sugary drinks hydrate too, but bring sugar you probably do not want.

## The simplest check

Pale-yellow urine and rarely feeling thirsty mean you are getting enough. Dark urine, headaches and tiredness suggest you are not.
