## The sums

- Pace = time ÷ distance
- Speed = distance ÷ time
- Predicted time = known time × (new distance ÷ known distance) raised to the power 1.06

That last line is Riegel's formula, published in 1981 and still the standard quick predictor. The 1.06 means you slow by about 6% each time the distance doubles.

**Example:** a 28-minute 5K predicts about 58:20 for 10K and 2:09 for a half marathon.

## Using your pace

| Run type | Pace compared with 5K pace |
| --- | --- |
| Easy and long runs | 60 to 90 seconds per km slower |
| Tempo | 15 to 25 seconds per km slower |
| Intervals | At or slightly faster |

Most of your running should be easy. If you cannot speak in full sentences, it is not an easy run.

## New to running?

Alternate one minute of jogging with two of walking for 20 minutes, three times a week, and lengthen the jogs gradually. Walking pace counts here too: enter a walk to see your speed.
