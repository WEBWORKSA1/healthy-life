## The rule in one line

Keep your waist to less than half your height.

**Example:** a 170 cm person with an 82 cm waist has a ratio of 82 ÷ 170 = 0.48, inside the healthy band.

## Why waist matters more than weight

Fat stored around the organs in your abdomen is more strongly linked with type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease than fat stored elsewhere. Two people with the same BMI can carry very different amounts of it. A tape measure picks that up; a scale cannot.

## The bands

| Ratio | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| 0.4 to 0.49 | Healthy central fat level |
| 0.5 to 0.59 | Increased health risk |
| 0.6 or more | High health risk |

The UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence recommends this measure for adults with a BMI under 35, and the same bands apply to men and women and across ethnic groups.

## How to measure

Stand, breathe out normally and wrap the tape midway between the bottom of your ribs and the top of your hip bones, roughly level with your navel. Keep it snug, not tight, and parallel to the floor.
