## The sum

Pure alcohol in grams = volume in ml × strength (% ABV) ÷ 100 × 0.789

**Example:** a 355 ml beer at 5%: 355 × 0.05 × 0.789 = 14 g, which is exactly one U.S. standard drink.

## One drink is not one drink

| Country | A standard drink or unit |
| --- | --- |
| United States | 14 g |
| Canada | 13.45 g |
| Australia | 10 g |
| United Kingdom | 8 g (one unit) |

A large glass of wine or a pint of strong beer is two to three UK units. Pouring at home, most people serve more than a standard measure.

## What the guidance says now

- **Canada (2023):** 2 drinks or fewer a week is low risk; 3 to 6 is moderate risk; 7 or more is increasingly high risk.
- **United Kingdom:** no more than 14 units a week, spread over 3 or more days.
- **United States (2025–2030 guidelines):** drink less for better health.
- **World Health Organization:** no amount is free of risk, particularly for cancer.

The direction is the same everywhere: less is better, and every reduction helps. Alcohol also costs sleep quality and adds calories: 7 kcal per gram, before any sugar in the drink.
