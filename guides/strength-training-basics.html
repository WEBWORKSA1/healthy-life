Strength training basics at any age

Why muscle matters more as you age, and a six-move routine you can do at home twice a week.

By the Healthy.Life editorial team. 4 minute read. Last reviewed 19 November 2026.

The short version

  • Work all major muscle groups on at least 2 days a week.
  • Six movements cover the whole body: squat, push, pull, hinge, press, carry.
  • The last 2 or 3 reps of a set should feel hard. That is the signal that builds strength.

Why it matters

From around 30, adults lose muscle slowly, and faster after 60. Strength work slows and partly reverses that. It also strengthens bones, improves blood sugar control, protects joints, reduces falls and makes everyday tasks easier. People who do it regularly live longer, independent of how much aerobic exercise they do.

Guidelines ask for muscle strengthening on at least 2 days a week. In the U.S., fewer than one adult in three does it.

Six movements, whole body

MovementAt homeWorks
SquatSit to stand from a chairThighs, hips
PushPush-up against a wall, then a counter, then the floorChest, arms, shoulders
PullRow with a resistance band or a loaded bagBack, arms
HingeHip bridge, then a deadlift with a bagHips, lower back, hamstrings
PressLift a weight overheadShoulders, arms
CarryWalk holding shopping bagsGrip, core

How much

  • Sets and reps: 1 to 3 sets of 8 to 12 repetitions.
  • Effort: stop when you could do about 2 more with good form.
  • Rest: a minute or two between sets; a day between sessions for the same muscles.
  • Time: 20 minutes covers it.

Getting stronger

When 12 reps feel comfortable, make it harder: a lower surface for push-ups, a heavier bag, a slower lowering phase, or an extra set. Small, steady increases are the whole method.

Staying safe

Breathe out on the effort; do not hold your breath. Move through a range that is comfortable. Muscle soreness a day or two later is normal at first. Sharp or joint pain is not: stop and adjust. If you have high blood pressure, a heart condition, osteoporosis or are pregnant, ask a professional how to adapt.

Protein helps

Muscle is built from protein. Most adults who train do well at 1.2 to 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day, spread across meals.

Questions people ask

Will lifting weights make me bulky?

No. Building large muscles takes years of heavy, specific training and eating. Twice-weekly strength work makes you firmer and stronger.

Am I too old to start?

No. Studies in people in their 80s and 90s show meaningful strength gains within weeks.
