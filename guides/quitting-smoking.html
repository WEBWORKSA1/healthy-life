Quitting smoking: what works

The methods with the best evidence, a week-by-week picture of withdrawal, and how to recover from a slip.

By the Healthy.Life editorial team. 5 minute read. Last reviewed 19 November 2026.

The short version

  • Medication plus support roughly doubles to triples your chance of quitting.
  • Cravings peak in the first week and each one passes in minutes.
  • Most people need several attempts. Every attempt teaches you something.

Why it is hard

Nicotine reaches the brain within seconds and triggers a release of dopamine. Over time the brain grows extra nicotine receptors, and going without causes withdrawal. Smoking also becomes wired into daily routines: coffee, driving, breaks, stress. Quitting means handling both the chemistry and the habits.

What works best

MethodWhat it is
Combination nicotine replacementA patch for a steady level plus gum, lozenge or spray for cravings
VareniclineA prescription tablet that reduces cravings and the reward from smoking
BupropionA prescription tablet that eases withdrawal
Behavioural supportA quitline, counsellor, group, text programme or app

Using a medicine and support together gives the highest success rates. Going "cold turkey" works for some people, but only about 3 to 5 in 100 unaided attempts last a year.

Nicotine e-cigarettes help some smokers quit and are recommended as a quitting aid in some countries, including the UK, and not in others. They are less harmful than smoking, not harmless, and the aim should be to stop those too.

Preparing

  1. Set a date within the next two weeks.
  2. Tell people and ask for their support.
  3. List your triggers and a plan for each.
  4. Get your medicine in advance; some start a week before quit day.
  5. Clear out cigarettes, lighters and ashtrays.

What to expect

  • Days 1 to 3: the strongest cravings, irritability, restlessness, trouble concentrating.
  • Weeks 1 to 4: cravings come less often. Sleep and mood can wobble. Appetite increases.
  • After a month: physical withdrawal is largely over. Cravings are occasional, usually set off by a situation.

Each craving lasts a few minutes. Delay, breathe slowly, drink water, move, or occupy your hands until it passes.

If you slip

A cigarette is a slip, not a relapse. Do not finish the pack. Work out what triggered it, adjust the plan and keep your quit date. Most former smokers needed more than one attempt.

The gains

Health benefits begin within minutes and continue for years; see the smoke-free calculator for the timeline. Money saved, better taste and smell, easier breathing and clearer skin arrive within weeks.

Help

Many countries run free quitlines and stop-smoking services. In the U.S., call 1-800-QUIT-NOW. Elsewhere, ask a pharmacist or doctor.

Questions people ask

What is the most effective way to quit smoking?

A stop-smoking medicine combined with behavioural support. Combination nicotine replacement and varenicline have the strongest evidence.

How long does nicotine withdrawal last?

The worst is over within a week, and most physical symptoms settle within two to four weeks.
