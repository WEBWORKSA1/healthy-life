Fibre: the nutrient most people miss

What fibre does, how much you need and an easy way to add 10 grams a day.

By the Healthy.Life editorial team. 4 minute read. Last reviewed 19 November 2026.

The short version

  • Adults need at least 25 to 30 g a day; most eat about half that.
  • It lowers cholesterol, steadies blood sugar and keeps your gut regular.
  • Add it gradually and drink more water as you do.

What it is

Fibre is the part of plant foods that your small intestine cannot digest. It reaches the large intestine, where it adds bulk and feeds gut bacteria.

  • Soluble fibre (oats, barley, beans, lentils, apples, citrus) forms a gel that slows digestion and binds cholesterol.
  • Insoluble fibre (whole wheat, bran, nuts, vegetable skins) adds bulk and keeps things moving.

Most plant foods contain both.

How much

BodyDaily amount for adults
World Health OrganizationAt least 25 g
United Kingdom30 g
United States14 g per 1,000 kcal: about 25 g for women, 38 g for men

Typical intake in Western countries is 15 to 20 g.

What it does for you

People who eat the most fibre have about 15 to 30% lower rates of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer than those who eat the least, according to a large review commissioned by the WHO. Fibre also helps you feel full, which makes weight easier to manage, and prevents constipation.

Where to find it

FoodFibre
Lentils or beans, 150 g cooked10 to 12 g
Bran or high-fibre cereal, 40 g8 to 10 g
Oats, 50 g dry5 g
Raspberries, 125 g8 g
Pear or apple with skin4 to 5 g
Wholemeal bread, 2 slices5 to 6 g
Almonds, 30 g3.5 g
Broccoli or carrots, 80 g2 to 3 g

Ten more grams, painlessly

  • Breakfast: oats or a wholegrain cereal with berries (+5 g).
  • Lunch: add half a can of beans or chickpeas (+6 g).
  • Snack: fruit with its skin and a few nuts (+5 g).

That is 16 g without changing dinner.

Go slowly

A sudden jump causes wind and bloating. Add about 5 g a day each week and drink more water. Symptoms settle as your gut adapts. If you have a bowel condition such as IBS or inflammatory bowel disease, ask a dietitian which types suit you.

Questions people ask

Do fibre supplements work?

Psyllium has good evidence for lowering cholesterol and easing constipation. Food sources bring other nutrients too, so use supplements as a top-up.

Does fibre help with weight loss?

It helps. High-fibre foods are filling for few calories, and higher intakes are linked with lower body weight over time.
