Health glossary

The terms you meet on lab reports, food labels and fitness trackers, explained in a sentence or two.

ABCDEFGHKLMOPRSTUVWZ

A

A1C
A blood test showing average blood glucose over about three months, given as a percentage. Under 5.7% is the normal range. Convert an A1C
Added sugars
Sugars and syrups put into food during processing or at the table. Sugar naturally present in whole fruit and plain milk is not included. Where sugar hides
Adequate intake
A recommended daily amount of a nutrient, set when there is not enough evidence for a precise requirement. Water and fibre targets are adequate intakes.
Aerobic activity
Movement that raises your heart rate and breathing for a sustained time, such as brisk walking, cycling or swimming. How much you need

B

Basal metabolic rate (BMR)
The energy your body uses at complete rest to stay alive, usually 60 to 70% of what you burn in a day. Calculate BMR
Blood pressure
The force of blood against artery walls, written as systolic over diastolic in mm Hg, for example 118/76. Check a reading
Body mass index (BMI)
Weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. A quick screening measure of weight for height. Calculate BMI
Box breathing
A paced breathing pattern: breathe in, hold, breathe out and hold again, each for four counts. Try it

C

Caffeine half-life
The time your body takes to clear half the caffeine from a dose: about 5 hours in most adults, with wide variation. Find your cut-off
Calorie (kcal)
The unit of energy used for food and activity. Food labels show kilocalories, commonly just called calories.
Carbohydrate
One of the three macronutrients, found in grains, fruit, vegetables, pulses and sugars. It provides 4 kcal per gram. Macro calculator
Cholesterol
A fatty substance carried in the blood. LDL is the type linked with artery disease; HDL helps carry cholesterol away. Know your numbers
Circadian rhythm
Your internal 24-hour clock, set mainly by light, that times sleep, alertness, hormones and body temperature. Sleep habits

D

Diastolic pressure
The bottom number in a blood pressure reading: the pressure in arteries while the heart rests between beats.
Dietary fibre
The parts of plant foods your body cannot digest. It supports bowel health, blood sugar control and cholesterol. Fibre guide

E

Estimated average glucose (eAG)
An A1C result translated into the same units a glucose meter shows. A1C converter

F

Free sugars
The WHO term for added sugars plus the sugars in honey, syrups and fruit juice. Added sugar guide

G

Glycaemic index
A ranking of how quickly a carbohydrate food raises blood glucose compared with pure glucose.

H

Heart-rate reserve
The gap between your maximum and resting heart rate. The Karvonen method sets training zones as percentages of it. Find your zones
Heart-rate zones
Bands of heart rate, usually five, that describe how hard you are working. Heart-rate training
Highly processed foods
Packaged products made mostly from refined ingredients and additives, such as soft drinks, sweets, crisps and many ready meals. Reading labels
Hypertension
The medical name for persistently high blood pressure: 130/80 mm Hg or higher under U.S. guidelines, 140/90 in many other countries. Measure at home

K

Karvonen method
A way of setting heart-rate zones using your resting heart rate as well as your maximum. Heart-rate zones

L

Lean body mass
Everything in your body that is not fat: muscle, bone, organs and water. Body fat calculator

M

Macronutrients
The nutrients you need in large amounts: protein, carbohydrate and fat. Macro calculator
Maximum heart rate
The fastest your heart can beat during all-out effort. It falls with age and is often estimated as 208 minus 0.7 times age. Estimate yours
MET
Metabolic equivalent of task. One MET is the energy used sitting quietly; brisk walking is about 4.3 METs. Calories burned
Mifflin-St Jeor equation
A widely used formula that estimates resting energy needs from weight, height, age and sex. BMR calculator
Moderate-intensity activity
Effort at which you can talk but not sing, roughly 3 to 6 METs. Check your week
Muscle-strengthening activity
Exercise that makes muscles work against resistance: weights, bands, body weight or heavy manual work. Strength basics

O

One-rep max (1RM)
The heaviest weight you can lift once with good form. Often estimated from a lighter set. Estimate 1RM

P

Pack-year
A measure of smoking history: packs smoked per day multiplied by years smoked. Checkup checklist
Prediabetes
Blood glucose higher than normal but below the diabetes threshold, for example an A1C of 5.7 to 6.4%. A1C converter
Protein
A macronutrient built from amino acids, needed to build and repair tissue. It provides 4 kcal per gram. Protein guide

R

RDA
Recommended Dietary Allowance: the daily intake that meets the needs of nearly all healthy people.
Resting heart rate
Your pulse when calm and rested, usually 60 to 100 beats a minute in adults and lower in fit people. Know your numbers

S

Saturated fat
A type of fat found mainly in fatty meat, butter, cheese, and coconut and palm oils. Guidelines advise keeping it under 10% of calories. Reading labels
Screening
A test for a disease in people with no symptoms, done to catch it early. Screenings by age
Sedentary time
Waking time spent sitting or lying down with very low energy use. Sitting less
Sleep cycle
A roughly 90-minute pass through light, deep and REM sleep. Most adults complete four to six a night. Sleep calculator
Sleep hygiene
The habits and bedroom conditions that support good sleep. Sleep habits
Sodium
The part of salt that affects blood pressure. One teaspoon of salt contains about 2,300 mg. Reading labels
Standard drink
A national measure of alcohol. It is 14 g of pure alcohol in the U.S., 13.45 g in Canada, 10 g in Australia and 8 g (one unit) in the UK. Alcohol calculator
Systolic pressure
The top number in a blood pressure reading: the pressure in arteries when the heart beats.

T

TDEE
Total daily energy expenditure: everything you burn in a day, from resting needs to movement and digestion. Daily calorie calculator
Trans fat
An industrially produced fat that raises heart-disease risk. Many countries have banned or restricted it.
Type 2 diabetes
A condition in which the body does not use insulin well and blood glucose stays too high. Know your numbers

U

Unsaturated fat
Fats found in olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado and oily fish. Replacing saturated fat with them lowers LDL cholesterol.
UV index
A scale of the strength of sunburn-causing ultraviolet light. Protection is advised at 3 and above. Sun safety

V

Vigorous-intensity activity
Effort at which you can say only a few words without pausing for breath, 6 METs or more. Check your week
VO2 max
The maximum rate at which your body can use oxygen during exercise; a measure of aerobic fitness. Heart-rate training

W

Waist-to-height ratio
Waist measurement divided by height. Keeping it under 0.5 is linked with lower health risk. Work out yours
Whole grains
Grains that keep all three parts of the kernel, such as oats, brown rice, whole wheat and barley. Balanced plate

Z

Zone 2
Easy aerobic effort, roughly 60 to 70% of maximum heart rate, at which you can hold a conversation. Heart-rate training
